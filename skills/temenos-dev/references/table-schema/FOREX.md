| 1 | `FX.DEAL.TYPE` | `Forex_DealType` | TField | No | Identifies the nature of the Foreign Exchange deal being processed. The application handles three different types: - SP or S = Spot - FW or F = Forward (Outright) - SW = Swap The following deal types are processed by the Foreign Exchange application: SP or S = Spot deals. Spot deals involve the immediate exchange of one currency for another and, because these are usually the most common in the market, this deal type has been selected as the system default. To allow sufficient time for the bank to check details and transfer funds, the accepted standard for spot deals is two business days following the date of the transaction. The system allows this, or any other market standard, to be defined on the FX.PARAMETERS table. The validation process will verify that the value date of the contract does not exceed the number of working days defined in the field SPOT.INTERNAL on the FX.PARAMETERS table. FW or F = Forward deals Forward contracts refer to the buying and selling of foreign currency at future exchange rates for delivery in the future, i.e. at a date greater than the locally defined spot date. The period varies but usually would be for one, two, three, six, and twelve months hence. When the value date of the contract exceeds the number of working days defined on the FX.PARAMETERS table in the field SPOT.INTERNAL, the contract will be considered as forward. SW = Swap deals. Swap deals involve the exchange of currencies at the spot rate with an agreement to reverse the transaction with an identical amount of currency at a later date at a specified rate. Contract types such as Overnight (ON), Tom-Next (TN) or Spot-Next (SN) are by-products of this deal type. To assist in entering the swap, details of the first leg - the spot leg - are carried forward to the subsequent -forward- leg. A contract cannot be established on a forward/forward basis. Validation Rules: 1 or 2 alpha characters deal type (Optional input; default = SP) Only the above mentioned deal types will be accepted. The field cannot be changed after authorisation of the contract. |

| 13 | `FX.FORWARD.RATE` | `Forex_ForwardRate` | TField | Yes | Identifies the exchange rate applicable to forward deals. The rate may be entered in full or as a 'premium-discount' over the spot rate. A '+' should be entered to signify a premium and a '-' for discount. The rate may also be derived using the FX margin functionality, where Treasury Rate and Margin Percentage are made available. Forward transactions are those with value dates further in the future than spot deals, usually for periods of one, two, three, six, and twelve months. These transactions involve an agreement to exchange currencies at an agreed rate on a specified future date. By entering into such a transaction, exchange risks can be avoided as the rate and settlement date remain the same regardless of what happens to the exchange rate. The actual forward rate, agreed at the time of the contract, is determined by the interest rates in the currencies concerned. For example, if dollar interest rates are 10% per annum and sterling 15%, it would be sensible to invest dollars in sterling and create a forward contract to re-purchase the dollars. However, the sterling forward exchange rate against dollar would be around 5% lower to reflect the interest rate difference between the two currencies. In the example, Sterling is worth less in the future in terms of dollars because it is the currency with the higher interest rate; sterling is said to be at a discount to dollars. As future dollars are worth more because of the interest rates differential, they are said to be at a premium in terms of sterling. The rate can be entered in this field as an actual forward exchange rate or as a premium/discount (identified with a preceeding + or -) which will then be added/subtracted to the spot rate defined in the previous field. If the spot rate is 2.00 and the forward rate 1.90, the forward rate can be entered as the 1.90 or as -.10 (i.e. a discount of .10 from the spot rate). Premium/discount is expressed as 'pips' which are defined on the Currency table for each currency. If, for example, +12 is entered as the premium on dollars and the Currency table shows a pip value of 4, the system will view this as a premium of 0.0012 to be added on the spot rate to derive the forward rate. Validation Rules: 1-12 type R (Standard Rate Format - up to 8 integer and 6 decimal) characters plus a decimal point based on SWIFT format or 1-15 type R (Up to 8 integer and 9 decimal) characters plus a decimal point when EXTENDED.PREC in SPF is set to Y. Or +/- NNNN where NNNN = Premium/Discount quoted in pips (see explanation in details). (Input is mandatory on forward deals or forward legs. Otherwise not allowed.) The rate must be in an acceptable format. The rate will be checked against the forward rate table and must be within the tolerance specified by the user on the currency table. If not, the operator will have to enter an override. The rate, amount bought, and amount sold must be complimentary.But, In case if the Treasury rate is entered with Margin Perc, only one amount is mandatory.
