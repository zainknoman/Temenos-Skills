| 20 | `FRD.FRA.TYPE` | `FraDeal_FraType` | TField | Yes | Specifies the underlying strategy of the FRA transaction. Two main types exist: HEDGE &lt;--------&gt; TRADING A distinction is made between the two in order to allow different accounting procedures to be implemented and for the timing of the recognition of profits and losses and as to when they are to be incorporated into the balance sheet. HEDGE Transactions Because the maturity dates of deposits and other sources of funds available in the market do not always sufficiently match the maturity of banks' assets, bankers make use of interest rate hedging instruments to reduce their interest gap exposure to acceptable limits. The FRAs have become today a popular alternative to Financial Futures as a means of hedging interest rate exposure. In order for a bank to enter into a FRA for hedging purposes, the following conditions will apply: 1. The bank must have net assets or liabilities which are subject to interest rate risk. This condition must exist after commitments and anticipated transactions reducing the risk have been taken into account. 2. The FRA transaction must be designated as a hedge and must reduce the interest rate risk on exposed assets and liabilities. As it can be seen, a conscious decision is done at deal level and since FRAs may be tailored into individual requirements, it is easier for such a trade to qualify as a hedge than for normal exchange governed Financial Futures. TRADING Transactions Apart from Hedging facilities, bankers will operate for other purposes such as general trading and arbitrage. Since such activities may be deemed to be of a more speculative nature, the concept of revaluing such positions is of importance. In order to allow the segregation of the above trade concepts, the input of trades requires the inclusion of the type of trade. This will allow the system to distinguish trades into their relevant categories both for accounting purposes and for revaluing. Validation Rules: H(edge), T(rading). Default none; Input is always mandatory. |
